Lesson 02 Spare That Spider

Posted on 2017-01-10 In English Reading time ≈ 5 mins.

How much of each year do spiders spend killing insects?

Why, you may wonder, should spiders be our friends? Because they destroy so many insects, and insects include some of the greatest enemies of the human race. Insects would make it impossible for us to live in the world; they would devour all our crops and kill our flocks and herds, if it were not for the protection we get from insect-eating animals. We owe a lot to the birds and beasts who eat insects but all of them put together kill only a fraction of the number destroyed by spiders. Moreover, unlike some of the other insect eaters, spiders never do the least harm to us or our belongings.

Spiders are not insects, as many people think, not even nearly related to them. One can tell the difference almost at a glance, for a spider always has eight legs and an insect never more than six.

How many spiders are engaged in this work on our behalf? One authority on spiders made a census of the spiders in a grass field in the south of England, and he estimated that there were more than 2,250,000 in one acre; that is something like 6,000,000 spiders of different kinds on a football pitch. Spiders are busy for at least half the year in killing insects. It is impossible to make more than the wildest guess at how many they kill, but they are hungry creatures, not content with only three meals a day. It has been estimated that the weight of all the insects destroyed by spiders in Britain in one year would be greater than the total weight of all the human beings in the country.

📝 文章大意

蜘蛛是人类的朋友，因为它们消灭了大量昆虫，而昆虫是人类最大的敌人之一：要不是有食虫动物保护，昆虫会吃光庄稼、咬死牲畜，让人类无法生存。鸟兽吃掉的昆虫加起来也只是蜘蛛消灭的一小部分，而且蜘蛛对人类毫无危害。蜘蛛并不是昆虫，一眼就能分辨：蜘蛛有八条腿，昆虫最多六条。一位专家统计，英格兰南部的草地上每英亩有 225 万多只蜘蛛，一年中至少有半年在捕食昆虫。据估计，英国的蜘蛛一年消灭的昆虫，重量超过全国人口的总重量。

标题问题的答案：蜘蛛每年至少有半年在捕食昆虫（at least half the year）。

📖 难句解析

1. Why, you may wonder, should spiders be our friends?

  • 主干是 Why should spiders be our friends?，you may wonder 是插入语
  • should 表示惊讶、不解的语气：（凭什么）竟然要……
  • 开篇设问，下一句 Because ... 直接作答
  • 译: 你可能会感到奇怪，蜘蛛为什么会是我们的朋友呢？

2. Insects would make it impossible for us to live in the world; they would devour all our crops and kill our flocks and herds, if it were not for the protection we get from insect-eating animals.

  • make it impossible for sb to do：it 是形式宾语，真正的宾语是 to live in the world
  • if it were not for ... = 要不是有……，与现在事实相反的虚拟语气，所以主句用 would make、would devour
  • 分号后的句子是对前半句的具体说明
  • we get from ... 是省略了 that 的定语从句，修饰 protection
  • 译: 要不是有食虫动物保护我们，昆虫会使我们无法在这个世界上生存；它们会吞食我们所有的庄稼，咬死我们的牛羊。

3. We owe a lot to the birds and beasts who eat insects but all of them put together kill only a fraction of the number destroyed by spiders.

  • owe a lot to sb = 多亏了某人，很大程度上归功于某人
  • all of them put together 是 all of them 加过去分词短语，put together = 加在一起
  • only a fraction of = 只是……的一小部分
  • destroyed by spiders 是过去分词作后置定语，修饰 the number
  • 译: 我们很感激吃昆虫的鸟兽，但它们捕食的昆虫全部加起来也只是蜘蛛消灭的昆虫数量的一小部分。

4. Moreover, unlike some of the other insect eaters, spiders never do the least harm to us or our belongings.

  • never ... the least = 一点也不，双重强调否定
  • do harm to sb = 对某人有害
  • 译: 而且，和别的食虫动物不同，蜘蛛对我们和我们的财物丝毫无害。

5. Spiders are not insects, as many people think, not even nearly related to them.

  • as many people think 修饰的是”蜘蛛是昆虫”这个观点，即”不像许多人以为的那样”，不是”正如人们所想”
  • not even nearly related to them 是省略句，= they are not even nearly related to insects
  • not even nearly = 甚至连近亲都算不上
  • 译: 蜘蛛并不像许多人以为的那样是昆虫，它们甚至与昆虫没有什么亲缘关系。

6. One can tell the difference almost at a glance, for a spider always has eight legs and an insect never more than six.

  • tell the difference = 区分，辨别
  • for 是并列连词，表示补充说明理由，比 because 更书面
  • an insect never more than six 省略了 has，= an insect never has more than six
  • 译: 人们几乎一眼就能看出它们的区别，因为蜘蛛总是八条腿，而昆虫的腿从来不超过六条。

7. … he estimated that there were more than 2,250,000 in one acre; that is something like 6,000,000 spiders of different kinds on a football pitch.

  • that is 指代前面整件事，表示换算：这相当于……
  • something like = 大约，相当于 about
  • 译: ……他估计每英亩草地上有 225 万多只蜘蛛，这就是说，一个足球场上大约有 600 万只各种各样的蜘蛛。

8. It is impossible to make more than the wildest guess at how many they kill, but they are hungry creatures, not content with only three meals a day.

  • it 是形式主语，真正主语是 to make ... guess
  • make a guess at = 猜测；impossible to make more than the wildest guess = 除了极粗略地猜一猜，什么也做不到
  • how many they kill 是宾语从句，作 at 的宾语
  • not content with ... 是形容词短语作 creatures 的补充说明
  • 译: 蜘蛛究竟消灭了多少昆虫，我们只能做非常粗略的猜测，但它们是贪吃的动物，不满足于一日三餐。

9. It has been estimated that the weight of all the insects destroyed by spiders in Britain in one year would be greater than the total weight of all the human beings in the country.

  • It has been estimated that ... = 据估计，it 是形式主语
  • 从句主干：the weight ... would be greater than the total weight ...
  • destroyed by spiders in Britain in one year 是过去分词短语作后置定语，修饰 insects
  • would 表示推测
  • 译: 据估计，英国的蜘蛛一年中消灭的昆虫的重量会超过全国人口的总重量。

Lesson 01 Finding Fossil Man

Posted on 2017-01-02 In English Reading time ≈ 5 mins.

Why are legends handed down by storytellers useful?

We can read of things that happened 5,000 years ago in the Near East, where people first learned to write. But there are some parts of the world where even now people cannot write. The only way that they can preserve their history is to recount it as sagas - legends handed down from one generation of storyteller to another. These legends are useful because they can tell us something about migrations of people who lived long ago, but none could write down what they did. Anthropologists wondered where the remote ancestors of the Polynesian peoples now living in the Pacific Islands came from. The sagas of these people explain that some of them came from Indonesia about 2,000 years ago.

But the first people who were like ourselves lived so long ago that even their sagas, if they had any, are forgotten. So archaeologists have neither history nor legends to help them find out where the first “modern men” came from.

Fortunately, however, ancient men made tools of stone, especially flint, because this is easier to shape than other kinds. They may also have used wood and skins, but these have rotted away. Stone does not decay, and so the tools of long ago have remained when even the bones of the men who made them have disappeared without trace.

📝 文章大意

我们能读到 5000 年前近东地区的文字记载，但世界上有些地方至今仍没有文字，那里的人只能靠代代相传的传说来保存历史。这些传说能告诉我们古代人类迁徙的情况，例如波利尼西亚人的传说说明，他们的一部分祖先大约 2000 年前来自印度尼西亚。然而最早的”现代人”生活的年代太久远，即使有过传说也早已失传，考古学家既没有历史记载也没有传说可以依靠。幸运的是，古人用石头（特别是燧石）制作工具，木头和兽皮早已腐烂，石头却不会腐烂，所以即使制造者的骨头都已消失，石器依然保存了下来。

标题问题的答案：传说能告诉我们很久以前人类迁徙的情况，而那些人自己并没有留下文字记载。

📖 难句解析

1. We can read of things that happened 5,000 years ago in the Near East, where people first learned to write.

  • read of = 读到（关于……的记载），比 read about 更书面
  • that happened ... 是限制性定语从句，修饰 things
  • where 引导非限制性定语从句，修饰 the Near East
  • 译: 我们可以读到 5000 年前在近东发生的事情，那里的人最早学会了书写。

2. The only way that they can preserve their history is to recount it as sagas - legends handed down from one generation of storyteller to another.

  • 主干：The only way ... is to do（唯一的办法是……），表语用不定式
  • that they can preserve their history 是定语从句修饰 way，that 可省略
  • recount it as sagas = 把它作为传说讲述出来；recount = 详细叙述
  • 破折号后的 legends ... 是 sagas 的同位语，解释什么是 saga
  • handed down 是过去分词作后置定语，表被动（= that are handed down）
  • from one generation of storyteller to another = 从一代讲述者传给下一代
  • 译: 他们保存历史的唯一方法是把历史当作传说讲述出来——这些传说由讲述者一代一代地传下去。

3. These legends are useful because they can tell us something about migrations of people who lived long ago, but none could write down what they did.

  • because 引导原因状语从句，内部又套 who lived long ago 修饰 people
  • but none could write down ... 的 none 指”那些人中没有一个”，补充说明为什么要靠传说
  • what they did 是宾语从句，作 write down 的宾语
  • 译: 这些传说之所以有用，是因为它们能告诉我们很久以前人类迁徙的情况，而那些人谁也不会把自己的经历写下来。

4. But the first people who were like ourselves lived so long ago that even their sagas, if they had any, are forgotten.

  • 主干：the first people ... lived so long ago that ...，so ... that = 如此……以至于
  • who were like ourselves 修饰 people，”像我们这样的人”即现代人
  • if they had any 是插入语，表示怀疑：就算他们有过传说
  • 译: 但是最早的和我们相似的人生活的年代太久远了，即使他们有过传说，也早已被遗忘了。

5. So archaeologists have neither history nor legends to help them find out where the first “modern men” came from.

  • neither A nor B = 既没有 A 也没有 B
  • to help them find out ... 是不定式作后置定语，修饰 history 和 legends；help sb do 省略 to
  • where ... came from 是宾语从句，用陈述语序
  • 译: 因此，考古学家既没有历史记载也没有传说来帮助他们弄清最早的”现代人”是从哪里来的。

6. Fortunately, however, ancient men made tools of stone, especially flint, because this is easier to shape than other kinds.

  • Fortunately, however 连用：however 表示与上段转折，fortunately 表达作者态度
  • this is easier to shape：主语 this（燧石）是 shape 的逻辑宾语，easy to do 结构里不定式用主动表被动
  • other kinds = other kinds of stone
  • 译: 然而幸运的是，古人用石头，特别是燧石制作工具，因为燧石比其他石头容易成形。

7. They may also have used wood and skins, but these have rotted away.

  • may have done = 可能已经做过，是对过去的推测
  • rot away = 烂掉，away 强调”完全消失”
  • 译: 他们也可能使用过木头和兽皮，但这些东西早已腐烂殆尽。

8. Stone does not decay, and so the tools of long ago have remained when even the bones of the men who made them have disappeared without trace.

  • the tools of long ago = 很久以前的工具，long ago 在这里当名词用，作 of 的宾语
  • when 不是”当……时”，而是”然而、尽管”，表对比（= even though）
  • even 强调连骨头都没留下，衬托石器保存之久
  • who made them 修饰 the men，them 指 tools
  • without trace = 无影无踪
  • 译: 石头不会腐烂，因此即使制造这些工具的人的骨头都已消失得无影无踪，远古时代的石器却保存了下来。

[LeetCode]201. Bitwise AND of Numbers Range

Posted on 2016-09-09 Reading time ≈ 1 mins.

Given a range [m, n] where 0 <= m <= n <= 2147483647, return the bitwise AND of all numbers in this range, inclusive.

For example, given the range [5, 7], you should return 4.

方法

直接对所有数按位与，会超时，因此只能采用别的方法。
对于[1,3], 对应二进制位01,010,011，按位与为0；对于[5,7]，对应二进制为0101,0110,0111，按位与为0100。
对于[m,n]，如果m!=n，那么m和n最右一位按位与必然为0；同时将m,n都右移一位，用bits记录移位数，如果m!=n，继续将m,n右移一位。最后m==n时，将m<<bits位即可，此时m可以为0或为其他值。如果m为0，n也为0,那么m和n的位数并不相同，因此结果为0；如果m不为0，那么m和n前几位必然相同，用m<<bits就可以得到最后结果。

[LeetCode]144. Binary Tree Preorder Traversal

Posted on 2016-06-03 Reading time ≈ 2 mins.

Given a binary tree, return the preorder traversal of its nodes’ values.

For example:
Given binary tree {1,#,2,3},

return [1,2,3].

Note: Recursive solution is trivial, could you do it iteratively?

方法

前序遍历二叉树，不用递归的方法，就需要借助栈了。
首先将root入栈，然后取出，先将右子节点压入栈，再讲左子节点压入栈。然后取出栈的节点，压入该节点的右、左子节点，循环直到栈为空即可。
